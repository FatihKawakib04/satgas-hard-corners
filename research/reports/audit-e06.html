Audit E06: BTC/ETH/SOL basket momentum

E06 is the only strategy that ever passed the lab gate. This page re-examines the three things that most often make backtest results look better than reality: a universe selected with hindsight, correction for multiple trials, and stability across periods.

Conclusion

E06 is downgraded from "pass" to "fail". Not because of a single p-value, but because of three mutually supporting pieces of evidence: its edge disappears once the universe is selected without hindsight, all of its profit is concentrated in 2020-2021 and 2023, and the effect vanishes when the data is sampled weekly. DSR itself cannot be used as the deciding factor because its result moves from 0.09 to 0.99 merely by changing the assumption about correlation between trials.

1. Survivorship bias

E06 selected BTC, ETH, and SOL. All three are still alive and have at some point risen hundreds of times over. That is a decision that could only be made after knowing the outcome. To test it, the universe is determined by a rule that does not look into the future: at the start of each month, take the 10 assets with the highest 30-day median dollar volume, with at least 180 days of history, then go long only those whose 60-day momentum is positive. The candidates include assets that are already dead or permanently collapsed (LUNA, FTT, SRM, WAVES, ANT, TORN, KLAY, BTS, XEM, REN).

With a realistic universe, this strategy does not make money: CAGR -2.8% and Sharpe 0.20, while buying BTC and then doing nothing yields 39.2% and Sharpe 0.87. The difference between rows two and three is small (0.20 vs 0.19), so what kills it is not the dead assets themselves, but the expansion of the universe: once we no longer pick the three winners, its edge disappears.

2. Audit Deflated Sharpe Ratio

DSR answers: after trying so many combinations, how confident are we that the best Sharpe is not luck. The formula follows Bailey and Lopez de Prado, and I have rechecked the implementation: SR0 uses the expected maximum of N normal trials, the denominator uses skewness and kurtosis, and Sharpe is calculated daily before being compared. What is problematic is not the formula, but its assumptions: trials are assumed to be independent, even though our 87 runs use the same data.

The return correlation between the four E06 lookbacks I measured: 0.74 on average (0.58 to 0.87). With those numbers, effective N falls to around 1.3, and DSR jumps to 0.99.

This means DSR is not final evidence in either direction. Under the independent assumption the result is 0.09 (fail), under the fully correlated assumption the result is 0.99 (pass). All that can be concluded is: E06's daily Sharpe (0.070) is below SR0 under the independent assumption (0.098) but above it under the correlated assumption. Therefore the decision on this page does not depend on DSR.

6. Limitations

  • There is no complete survivorship-bias-free universe dataset. The dead assets I could retrieve are only those still available on Binance; coins delisted from all exchanges and whose data is lost are not represented. Therefore the -2.8% figure is an upper bound of optimism, not a neutral estimate.
  • The LUNAUSDT ticker was reused for Terra 2.0 after May 2022, so the LUNA series is cut off on 2022-05-12. Splicing it together would create prices that never existed.
  • The null test in the previous validation used 81 permutations, so p=0.012 is the resolution floor (1/81), not the actual value. For finer claims, 1,000 permutations are needed.
  • DSR is sensitive to correlation assumptions and is not used as a deciding factor here.

7. Next steps

E06 is not continued as a candidate. Paper trading continues as an observation record, not as a strategy expected to be profitable. The next approach most worth testing is carry funding (E17): a strategy that does not depend on price direction and has different economics, with a walk-forward Sharpe of 2.30 that should actually be treated with suspicion because it is too good. The first test for E17 is to ensure its data is not leaked and its funding costs are real, not to add a new strategy.
