5. Why the marketplace is difficult

Today's data: Python/SQLite projects with budgets of 250 to 750 dollars have 147 bids, database automation projects at 15 to 25 dollars have 187 bids, and Azure integration projects have 65 bids. For accounts without reviews, the chance of winning at those numbers is small. Therefore the strategy used is not "send as many as possible", but:

  • send only to jobs whose scope can be explained in five lines and matches exactly the existing portfolio
  • start with small jobs that quickly produce the first review
  • build a niche position in crypto data pipelines, where there are fewer competitors

6. Product as a side business

Research pipeline as an installed service

What already exists and can be shown:

  • data collector running every 15 minutes with cache and source rotation
  • data quality audit: cross-check to exchanges, duplicate and gap detection
  • static report pages that withstand load and are cheap
  • pipeline health checks that detect silently dead jobs

What does not exist yet, and is more important than features:

  • evidence that someone is willing to pay
  • price
  • sales page
  • customer support

Validation plan before writing new code:

  1. 5 conversations with potential users: analysts, communities, or small teams that need daily data
  2. ask what they currently do manually and how many hours per week
  3. offer paid installation for the first 1 client with a 2-week scope
  4. do not write new features before one person says yes

Rule held to: no new features before one person pays or commits. A product with no buyers yet is a cost, not revenue.
